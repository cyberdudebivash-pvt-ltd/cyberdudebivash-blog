# REPORT TYPE — EXECUTIVE BRIEFING

**Version:** 1.0 · **Layer 3** · Inherits Constitution + Production Workflow
**Use when:** the audience is CISO / board / risk committee and the deliverable
is a decision brief, not a technical report.

---

## Objective
Enable an executive decision in under five minutes of reading. Translate
technical threat activity into business risk, priorities, and resource
decisions. Zero jargon without a plain-language anchor.

## Principles
- **Decision-first.** Lead with the decision and the "so what," then support it.
- **Business language.** Frame in operational, financial, compliance, and
  reputational terms — not CVE minutiae.
- **Honest and proportionate.** No fear-driven escalation; executives lose trust
  in analysts who cry wolf. Say when something is *not* urgent.

## Section structure (short, scannable)
1. **Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)** — 2–3 sentences: what happened, why it
   matters to *this* business, what decision is needed.
2. **Executive Risk Snapshot** — a small table: risk area → level → trend →
   recommended action.
3. **Strategic Assessment** — the significance in context: is this a shift, a
   spike, or business-as-usual?
4. **Business Impact** — operational / financial / compliance / reputation /
   supply-chain / identity / AI risk, only those that apply.
5. **Executive Priorities** — 3–5 ranked actions with owners and rough effort.
6. **What to Ask Your Team** — pointed questions the CISO can put to the SOC/
   architecture teams to verify exposure.
7. **Confidence & What Could Change This** — overall confidence with rationale;
   the intelligence gaps that would alter the assessment.
8. **References** (light) · **Analyst Conclusion** (one paragraph).

## Do not
- Bury the decision. · Use unexplained acronyms. · Present low-probability,
  high-drama scenarios as likely. · Recommend spend without articulating the risk
  it buys down.
